# World sketch: the Crystal War

An original steam-and-magic empire (PLAN D1). Every name here is ours; nothing is taken from a published game, book
or show. This is a sketch to build on, not canon yet. In-world, the machines are **crystalwork** (PLAN D8, D24).

## The premise
Under the continent of **Ardenmoor** runs the **Underroot**: a buried lattice of ancient roots turned to green glass.
They grow **crystal**: luminous teal stone that pushes up through the soil where a root runs close. Burned in a
furnace, crystal gives **crystal steam**, which drives engines harder than coal ever could; breathed in by a trained
**caster**, its vapour can be shaped into fire, frost, shields and light, at the price of warmth (casters run cold, and
their veins glow when they work).

Every nation keeps a **heart crystal**: one great stone, grown for centuries over the deepest root it can claim, that
feeds its engines and its casters. A nation whose crystal is shattered goes dark. In battle each side defends its own
crystal (the card game's 20-point target, PLAN D24).

The **Cindral Dominion** built an empire on crystal steam. Its **Great Crystal** at Vaskor, the largest ever grown,
now drinks the Underroot faster than it refills, and where a root dies the land above goes grey and dead: **the
Greying**. The Dominion says the Great Crystal keeps forty million people fed. The towns above the dying roots say it
is killing them. Both are right, and
that is the war.

Tone: serious and grounded. Soot, rivets, frost on brass, hard choices on every side. No side is purely villains.

## Factions
| Faction | Who they are | Look (house palette) | How they fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cindral Dominion** | The empire. Engine legions, rail artillery, caster officers. | Iron and ember: black iron, red lacquer, brass trim. | Crystalwork striders (two-legged walking engines with an enclosed cab), disciplined lines, artillery. |
| **The Free Gauge** | A rebel network of rail and mine towns, run by unions and runaway engineers. | Moss, patched canvas, scavenged brass. | Sabotage, stolen engines, ambush on the rails, repair under fire. |
| **The Sedgekin** | Tall, bark-skinned people who have lived along the surfacing roots since before the Dominion. | Verdigris, crystal teal, peat brown. | Root-singing (coaxing the Underroot itself), marsh ground, patience. |
| **The Gale Exchange** | Merchant sky-guilds on kite-borne airships. They sell to everyone. | Steam white, sky dusk, brass glint. | Airships, hired guns, bribes, information. |
| **The Ashen Choir** | Ascetics who believe crystal is a debt the world owes and must be burned away. | Ash grey, ember, white veils. | Fire casting, martyr charges, burning the ground they stand on. |
| **The Blackwick** (proposed, PLAN D12) | Crystal smugglers, fences and debt-collectors of Vaskor's undercity; they buy what the war spends. | Soot black, verdigris, the sickly green of crystal dissolved in acid. | Sacrifice, drain, fear and debts that tax the enemy. |

## Places
- **Vaskor**: the Dominion capital, a ring city of smokestacks inside an old caldera around the Great Crystal. At its
  centre the **Gear Citadel**, a stepped tower with a brass wheel on its face (the title screen's skyline).
- **Thrumhollow**: a mining town on the richest seam; the Free Gauge's heart. The ground hums when the pumps run.
- **The Rimeglass Flats**: a salt-white plain where a dead root left a field of shattered crystal; the war's main battlefield.
- **Kitebarrow**: a floating harbour moored to the hills by cables, the Gale Exchange's seat.
- **Sedgemere**: a marsh where the roots break the surface and glow at night; the Sedgekin's home.
- **The Hush**: a burned forest and the Ashen Choir's cloister.
- **The Greying**: the dead lands spreading out from Vaskor, grey grass and silent towns.

## Story spine (draft)
1. **The Northern Line.** A Free Gauge raid on a Dominion supply train goes wrong; Wren escapes with a strider's
   crystal core and a sealed survey from Archwright Venn.
2. **The Survey.** Venn's numbers say the Underroot dies in eleven years at the current draw, Dominion or no Dominion.
   Every faction wants the survey, for different reasons.
3. **The Rimeglass Flats.** The war's great battle. Who stands where depends on the player's choices.
4. **The Great Crystal.** The ending is a choice about the Great Crystal: starve it, shatter it, or bargain for time.

## Why each nation marches on Vaskor (PLAN D27)
The campaign (spec 20) runs Thrumhollow, the Rimeglass Flats, Vaskor for every nation; Venn's survey (eleven years) is
what sets all six marching. The full text, act lines and endings are in `src/story.ts`.
| Nation | Why it marches | Its ending |
|---|---|---|
| **Cindral Dominion** (Corrow) | The northern line is cut, the survey is loose, the Sovereign unseen and the city watch bought; the Marshal goes home to hold the Great Crystal. | Bargain: she cuts the draw by a third. |
| **The Free Gauge** (Wren) | Wren holds the survey; the union would sell it for a truce. She will read it from the Citadel steps. | The truth is out; the yards stop. |
| **The Sedgekin** (Ulla) | The Ninth Root under Sedgemere is going quiet; it runs to the Great Crystal. The clans go to sing it to sleep. | Starve: the Great Crystal is banked; the root answers. |
| **The Gale Exchange** (Quill) | The survey sets the price of crystal for eleven years, and Kitebarrow floats on crystal lift. Quill goes to sell time. | Bargain: half the draw, thirty years, a fee for Kitebarrow. |
| **The Ashen Choir** (Ebbe) | Eleven years is a date, not a warning; better one fire than the slow Greying. | Shatter: Vaskor goes dark. |
| **The Blackwick** (Vell) | The Dominion's war debts are secured on the Great Crystal's light; she calls them in before it goes out. | She owns it, and rations it. |

## In the card game (PLAN D4, D12)
The engine is Annals of Scrivenearth's, so the game is a hero-driven card battler against an AI. The six factions
above (the Blackwick included) are the six decks, matched to the engine's archetypes in PLAN D12, and the characters
become heroes that level up in play. Names, card text and art are all written fresh for Magitek Wars.
