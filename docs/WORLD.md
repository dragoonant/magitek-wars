# World sketch: the Gloam War

An original steam-and-magic empire (PLAN D1). Every name here is ours; nothing is taken from a published game, book
or show. This is a sketch to build on, not canon yet. In-world, the machines are **gloamwork** (PLAN D8).

## The premise
Under the continent of **Ardenmoor** runs the **Underroot**: a buried lattice of ancient roots turned to green glass.
They seep **gloam**, a luminous teal resin. Boiled, gloam gives **gloamsteam**, which drives engines harder than coal
ever could; breathed in by a trained **caster**, it can be shaped into fire, frost, shields and light, at the price of
warmth (casters run cold, and their veins glow when they work).

The **Cindral Dominion** built an empire on gloamsteam. Its Great Boilers now drink the Underroot faster than it
refills, and where a root dies the land above goes grey and dead: **the Greying**. The Dominion says the boilers
keep forty million people fed. The towns above the dying roots say the boilers are killing them. Both are right, and
that is the war.

Tone: serious and grounded. Soot, rivets, frost on brass, hard choices on every side. No side is purely villains.

## Factions
| Faction | Who they are | Look (house palette) | How they fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cindral Dominion** | The empire. Engine legions, rail artillery, caster officers. | Iron and ember: black iron, red lacquer, brass trim. | Gloamwork striders (two-legged walking boilers with an enclosed cab), disciplined lines, artillery. |
| **The Free Gauge** | A rebel network of rail and mine towns, run by unions and runaway engineers. | Moss, patched canvas, scavenged brass. | Sabotage, stolen engines, ambush on the rails, repair under fire. |
| **The Sedgekin** | Tall, bark-skinned people who have lived along the surfacing roots since before the Dominion. | Verdigris, gloam, peat brown. | Root-singing (coaxing the Underroot itself), marsh ground, patience. |
| **The Gale Exchange** | Merchant sky-guilds on kite-borne airships. They sell to everyone. | Steam white, sky dusk, brass glint. | Airships, hired guns, bribes, information. |
| **The Ashen Choir** | Ascetics who believe gloam is a debt the world owes and must be burned away. | Ash grey, ember, white veils. | Fire casting, martyr charges, burning the ground they stand on. |
| **The Blackwick** (proposed, PLAN D12) | Gloam smugglers, fences and debt-collectors of Vaskor's undercity; they buy what the war spends. | Soot black, verdigris, a sickly gloam green. | Sacrifice, drain, fear and debts that tax the enemy. |

## Places
- **Vaskor**: the Dominion capital, a ring city of smokestacks inside an old caldera around the Great Boilers. At its
  centre the **Gear Citadel**, a stepped tower with a brass wheel on its face (the title screen's skyline).
- **Thrumhollow**: a mining town on the richest seam; the Free Gauge's heart. The ground hums when the pumps run.
- **The Rimeglass Flats**: a salt-white plain where spilled gloam crystallised; the war's main battlefield.
- **Kitebarrow**: a floating harbour moored to the hills by cables, the Gale Exchange's seat.
- **Sedgemere**: a marsh where the roots break the surface and glow at night; the Sedgekin's home.
- **The Hush**: a burned forest and the Ashen Choir's cloister.
- **The Greying**: the dead lands spreading out from Vaskor, grey grass and silent towns.

## Story spine (draft)
1. **The Northern Line.** A Free Gauge raid on a Dominion supply train goes wrong; Wren escapes with a strider's
   gloam core and a sealed survey from Archwright Venn.
2. **The Survey.** Venn's numbers say the Underroot dies in eleven years at the current draw, Dominion or no Dominion.
   Every faction wants the survey, for different reasons.
3. **The Rimeglass Flats.** The war's great battle. Who stands where depends on the player's choices.
4. **The Boilers.** The ending is a choice about the Great Boilers: bank them, break them, or bargain for time.

## In the card game (PLAN D4, D12)
The engine is Annals of Scrivenearth's, so the game is a hero-driven card battler against an AI. The six factions
above (the Blackwick included) are the six decks, matched to the engine's archetypes in PLAN D12, and the characters
become heroes that level up in play. Names, card text and art are all written fresh for Magitek Wars.
